# App Store listing — copy to paste

The App Store is a search engine. Right now the listing targets none of the
things a sports fan actually types, so the app is invisible to people already
looking for exactly this.

Everything below goes into App Store Connect by hand. Character limits are
Apple's and are counted here.

---

## Subtitle — 30 characters, the highest-value field after the name

Apple indexes the subtitle for search, and it sits under the name in results.

**Use:**
```
Live game chat with your crew
```
*(29)*

Alternates, same length budget:
- `Game day group chat for fans` *(28)*
- `Watch the game with friends` *(27)*

Why not something cleverer: "digital tailgate" is your brand line, not a search
term. Nobody types it. The subtitle's job is to be found, not to be admired —
the tagline still leads on the website.

---

## Keywords — 100 characters, comma-separated, no spaces after commas

This is the field that actually moves search. Rules that matter:

- **Never repeat words already in your app name or subtitle.** Apple indexes
  those anyway, and a repeat wastes characters you cannot spare.
- No spaces after the commas — a space costs a character and buys nothing.
- Singular only. Apple matches plurals on its own.
- **No team or league trademarks.** "Browns", "Buckeyes", "NFL" as keywords is
  an easy rejection and, worse, a trademark complaint. The generic terms below
  reach the same searches without the exposure.

**Use:**
```
browns,buckeyes,bills,gamethread,tailgate,gameday,watchparty,cfb,alumni,rivalry,huddle,scores
```
*(99)*

Team names are in at your instruction. Stating the tradeoff once and then
leaving it alone: Apple has pulled apps for trademarked keywords, and a team can
complain directly. Three team names is also all 100 characters will hold, so
this buys visibility for three fanbases and none of the other hundred.

If a rejection comes back, the swap is immediate — replace the three names with
`football,college,fantasy` and resubmit. Nothing else in the listing changes.

The rest of the field is doing the same work without the exposure:
- `gamethread` — the exact phrase people type; Reddit owns it and you want it
- `tailgate`, `watchparty` — intent to watch WITH people
- `alumni` — the chapter audience searches this
- `cfb` — how college football fans actually abbreviate it

Rules that still apply:
- **Never repeat words from your app name or subtitle.** Apple indexes those
  already; a repeat wastes characters.
- No spaces after commas — a space costs a character and buys nothing.
- Singular only. Apple matches plurals itself.

## What NOT to do

- **Do not put team names in the app name.** It reads as trademark
  infringement and Apple has removed apps for less.
- **Do not chase "sports" alone.** Impossible to rank for, and the intent is
  worthless — someone typing "sports" is not looking for a group chat.
- **Do not rewrite the name.** You have reviews and installs attached to it, and
  the subtitle does the same job with none of the risk.

---

## Honest expectation

ASO is not a growth hack. It converts people already searching for something
like this, and there are not many of them per day. Expect single-digit installs
a week from search at your current visibility — real, free, compounding, small.

The reason to do it anyway: it costs one afternoon, it never stops working, and
right now the number is zero because nothing in the listing matches anything
anyone types.
