# Facebook group admins — the pitch

## Why this channel and not the group itself

Do not post in the group. Almost every fan group bans promotion, and a post
gets you removed by the same person you needed on your side.

**Message the admin.** Three things make this better than the email list you are
already working:

- **They are visible.** The admin's name is on the group. No list to buy, no
  address to guess.
- **Messenger, not email.** It sits in an inbox they actually read, and a reply
  is one tap.
- **It is often the same person.** The people running "Browns Backers of
  Charlotte" on Facebook are frequently the same people running the chapter you
  already emailed — warmer channel, same human.

## How to find them

Search Facebook for `Browns Backers`, `Bills Mafia`, `Buffs fans`, and so on.
Sort by members. The groups worth messaging have **2,000+ members and posts from
this week** — a 12,000-member group with nothing since March is a graveyard.

Open the group, click **Members**, and the admins are pinned at the top.

Do ten teams and you will have forty names. That is the whole list-building
step, and it takes an evening with no tooling.

## The message

Messenger is not email. Anything over four lines gets skimmed. Send this:

```
Hey — you run the Browns Backers group. I built a room that
calls the game live for Browns fans: score, big plays, the
clips, all landing on their own.

Would that be useful to your members Sunday? Happy to set one
up under the group's name — free, and you don't have to post
in it to keep it alive.
```

**Why it is shaped that way:**

- **Names their group in the first six words.** Proves it is not a blast.
- **Says what it does before what it is.** "Calls the game live" is a thing they
  can picture; "a group chat app" is not.
- **The ask is a question, not a link.** You want a reply, not a click — you
  have nothing per-group to link to yet.
- **"You don't have to post in it"** is the line that matters. Every group admin
  has run something that died from neglect. That sentence is the whole pitch.

## When they say yes

Set the room up, name it for their group, and send one link. Do not send a code,
do not send instructions, do not send a list of features.

## When they don't reply

Leave it. One follow-up a week later at most, and only for the big groups:

```
No worries if it's not for your group — last note from me.
The room's there if you ever want it for a game day.
```

## What to expect

Most will not answer. Ten admins might produce one or two conversations, and
one of those might set a room up. That is not a bad result — it is the same
shape as the chapter emails, with a much better reply rate because it is a
person's inbox rather than a club address.

Track them in the Chapters tab as you go so you can see who is outstanding.
