# Side Huddle — The Brief

*One page. Everything else in this folder derives from it.*

---

## What are we

**The group chat for the game.** Your people, in a room, while it's happening —
in a room that already knows what's on.

We are not a social network (no strangers, no algorithmic feed), not a scores app
(ESPN has that), and not a general messenger (iMessage owns that). We are the
thing you open **instead of X during the game.**

> **Never watch a game alone.**

---

## Target audience

**The unit is not a person. It is a group of people who already know each other.**
*(No size cap — not on friend huddles, not on verified ones.)*

| | Who | Where they are now |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | 18–34 fans who watch alone on a couch with a phone in their hand | X/Twitter during the game, a group text where nobody's watching |
| **The group** | Friends, tailgate crew, family, coworkers — an existing real-world group | Scattered across iMessage, GroupMe, WhatsApp |
| **Secondary** | Fan communities with no home — alumni chapters, fan clubs, Facebook groups | Facebook Groups, email lists, nothing |

We do not acquire users. **We acquire groups.** One person brings four.

---

## Goal

**The de facto gameday app for fans and friends.**

Success is not DAU. Success is: *when a game starts, the room is already open.*

---

## The experience

**A digital tailgate. Snap meets ESPN.**

| From Snap | From ESPN |
|---|---|
| Speed — low-effort input, no composing | It knows the game: score, clock, down, play |
| Reactions and camera as the default | News, clips, highlights |
| The moment, not the archive | Authority — the facts are real |

Snap won by changing the primitive: it opens to a camera, not a keyboard. Our
primitive is **the reaction at the moment of the play.** Text stays — speed in a
group chat is the point — but it is beside the reactions, not above them.

---

## What we offer that chats don't

1. **Shared synchronized context.** Everyone is on the same play at the same
   second. iMessage has no shared clock and no shared subject — this is the one
   structural thing it cannot copy at any price.
2. **Simultaneity as an object.** *"6 of you · same second"* on the same play.
   Not six notifications — one event.
3. **Reactions, instant video, photos** from the moment, at tap-speed.
4. **Community past your contact list.** A group text is sealed at five people.
   A huddle grows by one hop — a friend's cousin, an alumni chapter, a verified room.
5. **The room knows the game.** Score, clock, news — without anyone typing it.
6. **@coach** — an expert on this team, this room, this game. Press to talk or type.

**The competition is ESPN, X, Snap — and chat itself.**

We *are* an enhanced chat. Snap proved a group chat will move when the new one
offers something the old one structurally cannot — it moved millions off iMessage
by changing the primitive from a sentence to a camera. We are making the same
bet with the reaction, the shared clock, and a room that knows the game.

The bet is not "your group chat, but slightly better." It is "your group chat
cannot do this at all."

## The role of the bot

The bot is the **spine, not the content.** The room's substance comes from the
people in it. Three distinct jobs:

### 1. In-game — the spine
Calls **scoring plays and little else.** Quiet stretches between scores are
intended, not a bug. Every message is something to react to, never filler.
More bot chatter competes with the room instead of prompting it.

### 2. Around the game (all week) — the feed
**Social-media news, not a newspaper.** X posts, fan reaction, clips, highlights,
what the fanbase is actually arguing about. Not beat-writer RSS.

This is what replaces the X timeline, and it is why the room is alive on a
Tuesday. **It is also why a brand-new room is never empty** — six people, zero
messages, and the room already has something in it.

### 3. @coach — on demand only
Answers when asked, never volunteers. Expert on the **team, the room, and the
game** — record, roster, box score, the room's own history and ledger. Press to
talk or type. Lives in the composer, not the nav bar.

### Non-negotiable rules
- Every fact the bot states is reproducible from a table. It rephrases facts; it
  never retrieves or invents them.
- It never narrates a game that isn't happening.
- It has a **side** — it is a fan of one team, not a neutral scoreboard. That is
  the entertainment. *(See open question in `05-open-questions.md`.)*
